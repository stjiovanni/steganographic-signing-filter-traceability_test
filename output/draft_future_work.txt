Future Work: Geometric Robustness

The experimental results demonstrate that geometric transforms represent the most critical vulnerability in the evaluated watermarking and hashing pipelines. TrustMark's raw bit accuracy drops to near-random-guessing levels under 90-degree rotation (mean 51.3 percent) and 50 percent centre crop (mean 53.7 percent), with worst-case values as low as 35 percent and 38 percent respectively. Perceptual hashes fare no better: PDQ exhibits 50.3 percent bit change at 90-degree rotation and 49.7 percent at 50 percent centre crop, both effectively indistinguishable from random noise over its 256-bit output. Even pHash, which is designed for partial invariance, loses over 22 percent of its bits under the same conditions.

These findings are consistent with the broader literature, which identifies DCT-based representations as inherently vulnerable to spatial reordering (McKeown and Buchanan, 2023; Jain et al., 2022). A promising mitigation is a synchronisation-layer augmentation placed before the decoding stage. Feature-based image registration methods such as SIFT or ORB keypoint detection could estimate the geometric transformation applied to the watermarked image, re-align it to the original coordinate frame, and thereby restore decoder accuracy. Integrating such a pre-processing step would extend the pipeline's resilience beyond the currently tested affine and photometric attacks, addressing the gap that geometric robustness leaves open.
