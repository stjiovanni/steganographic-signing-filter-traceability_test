## TrustMark

TrustMark is a deep learning-based image watermarking method.

- **Repository**: https://github.com/adobe/trustmark
- **PyPI**: https://pypi.org/project/trustmark/
- **License**: MIT
- **Paper**: Bui, T., Agarwal, S. and Collomosse, J. (2025) 'TrustMark: Robust Watermarking and Watermark Removal for Arbitrary Resolution Images', *Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision*, pp. 18629-18639. Available at: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Bui_TrustMark_Robust_Watermarking_and_Watermark_Removal_for_Arbitrary_Resolution_Images_ICCV_2025_paper.html
- **Version used**: 0.9.1

The repository and PyPI package are software records, not substitutes for the published paper citation.

## Additional 2016-2017 peer-reviewed sources

These sources are archival journal or published proceedings records, not preprints. The DOI links are the official publisher/proceedings landing pages.

- **[R23 | 2.2 Robust/reversible watermarking; 2.6 Gap and contribution]** Ansari, I. A., Pant, M. and Ahn, C. W. (2016) 'Artificial bee colony optimized robust-reversible image watermarking', *Multimedia Tools and Applications*, 76(17), pp. 18001-18025. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11042-016-3680-z. Relevance: establishes an optimization-based robust and reversible watermarking design, relevant to the trade-off between payload recovery, image quality, and reversibility. Implementable gap: add a key-dependent reversible DCT/DWT baseline and compare PSNR, BER, and exact host recovery against the current DCT baseline under the same 12 transforms; the project currently has no reversibility or optimization objective.
- **[R24 | 2.2 Robust authentication; 2.6 Gap and contribution]** Kulkarni, P. R., Mulani, A. O. and Mane, P. B. (2016) 'Robust Invisible Watermarking for Image Authentication', *Emerging Trends in Electrical, Communications and Information Technologies*, pp. 193-200. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-10-1540-3_20. Relevance: connects invisible robust embedding with image authentication rather than only payload survival. Implementable gap: evaluate authentication as a separate tamper-localisation or integrity metric, instead of treating recovered bits alone as provenance evidence; the current benchmark has no localisation output.
- **[R25 | 2.2 Transform-domain watermarking; 2.8 Experimental design]** Pardhu, T. and Perli, B. R. (2016) 'Digital image watermarking in frequency domain', *2016 International Conference on Communication and Signal Processing (ICCSP)*, pp. 208-211. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCSP.2016.7754123. Relevance: provides a peer-reviewed frequency-domain comparison point for the project’s differential mid-frequency DCT baseline. Implementable gap: reproduce a second frequency-domain embedding rule with an explicit attack-calibrated strength sweep, then report the robustness-quality frontier rather than one fixed embedding strength.
- **[R26 | 2.2 Robust transform-domain watermarking; 2.6 Gap and contribution]** Ansari, I. A., Pant, M. and Ahn, C. W. (2017) 'Secured and Optimized Robust Image Watermarking Scheme', *Arabian Journal for Science and Engineering*, 43(8), pp. 4085-4104. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13369-017-2777-7. Relevance: links robustness with security and parameter optimization, useful for separating survivability under benign transforms from resistance to intentional removal. Implementable gap: add seeded/keyed embedding and adaptive attack testing; the current experiment uses fixed payloads and isolated, non-adaptive transformations.
- **[R27 | 2.2 Transform-domain watermarking; 2.8 Experimental design]** Sharma, R., Gupta, A. K., Singh, D., Verma, V. S. and Bhardwaj, A. (2017) 'A robust image watermarking in contourlet transform domain', *AIP Conference Proceedings*, 1897, article 020014. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5008693. Relevance: supplies a published multiscale transform-domain alternative to DCT, especially relevant to the project’s poor performance on geometric and crop conditions. Implementable gap: implement contourlet or a documented multiscale substitute and test whether directional subbands improve crop/rotation recovery, while keeping the current transform matrix and metrics unchanged.
